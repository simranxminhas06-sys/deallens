## Section 1: Business Description

Amazon.com, Inc. operates as an online retailer and technology company across two
primary customer-facing segments: North America and International retail
e-commerce, plus Amazon Web Services (AWS), a cloud infrastructure business. The
company's stated mission is to be "Earth's most customer-centric company," with a
long-standing strategy of reinvesting operating cash flow into new growth areas
rather than maximizing near-term profit. By 2016, Amazon had begun experimenting
with physical retail formats, including Amazon Books stores and an early pilot of
a cashier-less convenience store concept (Amazon Go), alongside its existing
AmazonFresh online grocery delivery service in a limited number of metro markets.

## Section 2: Financial Highlights (Fiscal Year 2016)

- Net sales: approximately $136.0 billion, up roughly 27% year over year.
- North America segment net sales: approximately $80.0 billion.
- International segment net sales: approximately $43.0 billion.
- AWS segment net sales: approximately $12.2 billion, with segment operating
  income of approximately $3.1 billion.
- Consolidated operating income: approximately $4.2 billion (operating margin of
  roughly 3%), reflecting continued heavy investment in fulfillment capacity,
  video content, and international expansion.
- Employees: approximately 341,000 full-time and part-time employees worldwide
  (excluding contractors and temporary personnel) as of fiscal year-end 2016.

## Section 3: Strategic Priorities Disclosed to Investors

- Continued build-out of same-day and next-day fulfillment infrastructure to
  shorten delivery times, particularly for Prime members.
- Growth of the Amazon Prime membership program as a driver of customer
  retention and purchase frequency.
- Selective investment in physical retail formats to complement e-commerce,
  citing grocery as a large addressable category still underpenetrated online.
- Continued investment in private-label and exclusive brands across categories.
- Expansion of AWS as a primary profit engine funding investment in the retail
  business.

## Section 4: Risk Factors (Selected)

- Intense competition from other retailers, including in the grocery category,
  where incumbents have extensive physical distribution networks Amazon lacks.
- Thin retail margins and a history of prioritizing growth and market share over
  near-term profitability.
- Execution risk in scaling new physical-format businesses, which require
  different operational competencies (perishables logistics, in-store labor
  management) than Amazon's existing e-commerce operations.
