# Transaction Assumptions — Amazon / Whole Foods Market

_Simplified, synthetic summary of publicly reported deal terms, prepared for the
DealLens demo._

## Section 1: Deal Terms

- Acquirer: Amazon.com, Inc.
- Target: Whole Foods Market, Inc.
- Announcement date: June 16, 2017.
- Structure: All-cash tender offer / merger, approximately $42.00 per share.
- Total transaction value: approximately $13.7 billion, including assumed debt.
- Closing date: August 28, 2017 (approximately 2.5 months from announcement,
  a relatively fast regulatory review given limited direct market overlap).

## Section 2: Stated Deal Rationale (Public Commentary at Announcement)

- Amazon framed the deal as accelerating its push into physical grocery retail
  and reaching customers who prefer shopping for groceries in stores.
- Whole Foods' urban, affluent-market real estate footprint was cited by
  outside analysts as valuable for last-mile grocery delivery and as physical
  hubs for Amazon Lockers and returns.
- Whole Foods leadership (CEO John Mackey) publicly framed the deal as a way to
  accelerate innovation and lower prices for customers while preserving the
  brand's quality standards.

## Section 3: Known Post-Close Actions (For Comparison Against Generated Plan)

_Use this section only to sanity-check the agent's 100-day plan against what
actually happened — do not feed it into the analysis as an input document._

- Amazon cut prices on a range of staple items at Whole Foods stores on the
  first day of closing.
- Amazon Lockers were installed in Whole Foods stores for package pickup and
  returns.
- Whole Foods products (365 Everyday Value private label) were made available
  on Amazon.com.
- Amazon Prime was integrated as Whole Foods' loyalty program in 2018, offering
  member-only discounts and cash back on purchases.
- The "365 by Whole Foods Market" smaller-format store concept was wound down
  in 2019 as Amazon consolidated its physical-grocery strategy around the core
  Whole Foods banner and, later, a separate Amazon Fresh store format.
- Reports in subsequent years described cultural friction between Whole Foods'
  historically decentralized, team-based store culture and Amazon's centralized,
  metrics-driven operating style.
